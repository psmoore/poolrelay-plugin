# Finding a pool's lane count

Search in this order. Stop when a source states the count outright.

1. **The facility's own pages.** Look at the aquatics page, rental pages and "about the pool" text. Phrases to
   look for: "8-lane", "25-yard", "50-meter", "long course", "short course", "bulkhead".
2. **Meet information.** Swim meet announcements (USA Swimming, US Masters Swimming, high school) describe the
   competition pool precisely. For example, "50-meter x 25-yard pool with eight lanes, 9 ft wide; adjacent
   25-yard warm-up pool with six lanes".
3. **Pool directories.** places2swim.com lists length and lanes for most US pools. Its "pool" line reads like
   "50m, 8 lanes x 25y, 20 lanes": that means 8 long-course lanes OR 20 short-course lanes, not 28.
4. **Club pages.** Swim clubs describe the pools they train in, sometimes inaccurately. Weigh them below the
   facility and meet info.
5. **Photos and news.** A clear photo of the lane lines, or a construction or renovation article.

## When sources disagree

Use the most authoritative source (facility or meet info over a directory, and a directory over a club page).
Record the disagreement as a question for the staff.

## When nothing is found

Use the standard and say so:

| Pool | Standard |
|---|---|
| 25-yard or 25 m pool | 8 or 10 lanes. Pick 8 unless the pool is clearly large. |
| 50 m pool | Two configurations: Long Course, 8 lanes, and Short Course, 20 lanes |
| Shallow or teaching pool | Often 3–4 lanes when it has lanes at all |
| Splash pad, play pool, zero-entry area | No lanes. Book on the pool itself. |

## A person on site beats every source

Staff and lifeguards know the real layout, including which pools exist, which are closed and how programs
share the water. When someone reports what the staff said, update the model to match. Move bookings; don't
recreate them.
